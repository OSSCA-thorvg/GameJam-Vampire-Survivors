# 0025. Fill the screen with a cropped view on touch devices

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-11-10
- Related issues: #171; amends ADR [0004](0004-run-game-in-dedicated-web-worker.md) (only how the fixed logical view is presented in touch mode; the view itself, the threads and the fixed step are retained) and moves ADR [0024](0024-show-the-opponent-as-picture-in-picture-on-touch-devices.md)'s inset offsets to the visible area's edges

## Context

- REQUIREMENTS 12 and ADR 0004: the game shows a fixed 1280×720 logical view, "scaled to fit and letterboxed, so window size gives no advantage in versus play". #149 made the game playable by touch and #150 (ADR 0024) shows the versus opponent as a picture-in-picture inset in touch mode.
- A phone in landscape is wider than 16:9 (a Pixel 7 is 863×360 CSS pixels in Chrome, an iPhone 14 844×390), so the letterboxed view left black bars on both sides and the characters were small: on 844×390 the view was 693×390.
- **User decision (2026-11-10):** "on mobile the game screen is too small; can it fill the screen, even if fewer monsters are visible?" Offered fill only, 1.5× zoom and 2× zoom, the user chose **fill only**: the view covers the screen with no extra zoom, and seeing a little less is accepted.
- **User decision (2026-11-10), after seeing a 4:3 screenshot:** the cover fit never crops the visible width below a floor derived from the HUD's own extents; a narrower touch window keeps bars above and below instead of an overlapping HUD.
- ADR 0003: no Paint is created per frame or per layout change. ADR 0005: both players of a match face the same schedule, so the simulation must not depend on the screen.

## Decision

- **Cover fit in touch mode.** `ViewFit` is `'contain' | 'cover'` (`main/viewport.ts`). `computeViewport(w, h, dpr, panes, mode, fit)` defaults to `contain`, which is the letterbox of ADR 0004, unchanged. The main thread asks for `cover` while `inputMode.mode === 'touch'` (`main/index.ts`), so desktop keeps the letterbox and a switch of input mode switches the fit at once, like the pane mode of ADR 0024 (`main/resize.ts`: a fit change is never debounced).
- **What cover does.** It applies only when the fitted area is one view (layout `single` or `pip`); two equal panes (`columns`, `rows`) stay letterboxed. The canvas is the whole window in whole device pixels; `scale = max(width / 1280, height / 720)` of the backing size; the overflowing axis is cropped evenly, by a whole number of device pixels on each side, so pixel art stays on the device grid. `Viewport.visible` is the part of the view that is shown, in logical units: `{ x: cropX / scale, y: cropY / scale, width: width / scale, height: height / scale }`; with `contain` it is the whole view `{ 0, 0, 1280, 720 }`. On 844×390 the whole width and 591.5 of the 720 units of height are visible (about 1.2× larger).
- **The floor.** The crop never leaves less than `MIN_VISIBLE_WIDTH` = 1220 logical units of width (`worker/constants.ts`). When covering the window would show less (a window narrower than 1220:720, such as a 4:3 tablet), the scale is `width / 1220` instead, exactly 1220 units are visible (30 cropped on each side), the whole height of the view fits, and the canvas is the fitted box: as wide as the window and letterboxed above and below, like `contain`. `fit` is still `cover`. On 1024×768 the canvas is 1024×604 between two 82 CSS pixel bars, about 5 % larger than the plain letterbox. The floor is derived, not chosen (`worker/render/hud-extents.ts`): it is the smallest multiple of 20 at which four pairs of the HUD's top row stay at least 8 units apart, measured with the longest weapon or passive display name plus " Lv 8", the timer's `mm:ss` and the widest "Sent <label>" toast at 0.8 of the font size per glyph: the inventory (its backing, 466 units from the left, or its longest row) to the timer; the inventory to the sent toast; and, in `pip`, the timer and the toast to the inset's backdrop. The inventory-to-toast pair sets the value (1216, rounded up); without it the floor would be 1140. `hud-extents.test.ts` fails if `MIN_VISIBLE_WIDTH` is not the derived width or any pair comes within 8 units at it, so a longer item name or a wider HUD moves the floor instead of overlapping.
- **The simulation's view is unchanged.** `camera.viewRect`, the spawner's ring, culling, relocation, the ground grid, the view stream and the protocol stay on the full 1280×720 view. `visible` crosses to the worker in `init` and `resize` and is read only by rendering code. A player on a cropped screen sees less than the simulation's view, never more, so no screen gives an advantage; that is the property ADR 0004's letterbox line protected.
- **Worker.** The root scene is scaled as before and translated by `(-visible.x × scale, -visible.y × scale)` device pixels (`render/thorvg.ts` `placeRoot`), so the visible part starts at the canvas origin. The own pane's clip is the visible area. Still one render per resize (#165), and no Paint is created or disposed: clips are rewritten in place and HUD paints are moved.
- **HUD anchoring.** Every view-space HUD element keeps its offset from one edge group of `visible` (`render/hud.ts` `hudAnchors`, applied by `Hud.layout(visible)` on a layout change, never per frame): the inventory from the top left; the timer and the sent toast from the top centre; "Lv n", the skull and the kill count from the top right; the XP bar spans the visible width. The perf overlay follows the top left. The HP bar stays in world space under the player, who is at the centre of the visible area because the crop is even. The defeat line and the frost overlay are centred on or cover the full view and need no change.
- **The inset (ADR 0024).** Its rect is measured from the visible area: `x = visible.x + visible.width − 16 − 409.6`, `y = visible.y + 64` (`worker/constants.ts` `pipRect`, shared by `main/viewport.ts` and `render/panes.ts`). The 12-unit gap to the kill count holds because both move by the same offsets. It still shows the opponent's whole 1280×720 view.
- **DOM.** `Viewport.panes[0]` is the canvas box with `cover` (also when the floor keeps bars), so `--self-*` and `--opp-*` keep working. The touch pause button is at the window's top right, which the letterbox left outside the view on phones; over a covered view it would lie on the kill count, so it starts 8 units under the kill count instead, measured from the canvas's top (`hudClearCss`, the `--hud-clear` CSS variable). The held boot loading text is placed through `visible` and ends at the visible bottom edge at the latest.
- **Boot splash.** It stays centred on the full view. Its drawn content (449..833 × 150..561 at 1280×720) lies inside the visible area on phones and 4:3 tablets, so only empty margins are cropped.

## Alternatives considered

- **Keep the letterbox on phones** — what the user reported: small characters and unused screen.
- **Extra zoom (1.5× or 2×)** — offered and declined by the user; it hides much more of the world and lets enemies reach the player sooner after appearing.
- **Change the logical view to the screen's aspect ratio** — the spawn ring, culling and the view stream would differ per device, so two players of a match would no longer face the same schedule and a wide screen would see more (ADR 0004, ADR 0005).
- **Choose the fit by screen size or aspect ratio instead of input mode** — a small desktop window would lose the letterbox, and the touch controls, the inset and the fit would follow different rules; #149 and ADR 0024 already decide by input mode.
- **Scale the HUD down to fit instead of moving it** — the texts are already small on a phone; moving keeps their size.
- **Cover narrow windows fully and accept or rearrange the HUD** — at 4:3 only 960 units of width are visible: the inventory ran into the timer, and in a match the timer touched the inset and the sent toast overlapped it. A compact second HUD layout was more code and another thing to verify than a floor on the crop, and tablets are not the target device.
- **A hand-picked floor** — it would go stale with the next longer item name; the derived floor is checked by a test.

## Consequences

- Phones show the game edge to edge, about 1.2× larger on a 844×390 screen; what is cropped is the top and bottom of the view (the left and right on screens narrower than 16:9).
- Enemies still spawn around the full view, so on a cropped screen they appear from just outside the screen a little later than they spawn. Nothing in the balance changes.
- The layout now depends on the input mode in single player too: a mouse click or a movement key on a touch device brings the letterbox back, and a touch covers again. Each switch costs one canvas resize and one render.
- Every view-space HUD element must be placed through `hudAnchors`; a new element fixed to 1280×720 coordinates would be cropped in touch mode.
- Narrow touch windows keep bars: anything narrower than 1220:720 (about 1.69:1), such as a 4:3 tablet or a square foldable, is cropped only 30 units on each side and letterboxed above and below, so it gains about 5 % in size over the plain letterbox rather than filling the screen. Phones are wider than 16:9 and are covered completely.
- The floor follows the HUD: a longer display name, a wider inventory or a wider toast raises `MIN_VISIBLE_WIDTH` through the failing test, up to 1280, where the cover fit crops no width at all. Revisit with a compact HUD if 4:3 touch devices should fill the screen.
- Revisit if players ask for a zoom setting, or if cover fit should also be offered on desktop.
